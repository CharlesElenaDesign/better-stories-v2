Act one · the prior attempt, without blame

The AI Factory didn’t fail on technology

It was designed without the slowest step in it. That is a different problem, and a fixable one.

It was stood up in 2023, it built what it said it would build, and it stopped at approval. The business units then did the rational thing and went back to building their own — which is where a large share of the four hundred came from.

Nobody in that sequence made a mistake. A design that excludes the slowest step will always look fast right up until it ships.

We are saying this plainly because the alternative reading — that the programme or its sponsor underdelivered — is both wrong and expensive. It is what stops the second attempt getting funded.

2023

Stood up, delivered its scope, and stalled at the same gate that is holding the queue today.

What it was asked for

A central platform and a centre of excellence, to stop every business unit solving the same problem twice.

What it delivered

Exactly that. Platform, patterns, people. On the evidence available, on scope.

What was not in scope

The model-risk evidence step, and the throughput of the desk that owns it.

What happened next

The business units re-federated. Volume went up, clearances did not.

How this document was made

Three agents, a person between each, and one brief nobody re-typed.

find-the-story

Six questions against the account brief. Returned one headline, two alternatives, the facts it rests on, and what the headline costs. Wrote no copy. Flagged its own two gaps — they are pages 07 and 12.

structure-the-narrative

Took the headline and the state of the deal. Returned three acts and sixteen pages — the job each page does and the single sentence it must land. Did not reopen the story. Cut a contents page nobody would have missed.

theme-it

Five decisions: territory, colour, type, imagery, motion. Wrote the image prompt below, set the accent rule this document obeys — once per page, only on the number that is the argument — and chose one motion, an upward fade.

Seven frames were generated as one set from the approved cover, at the ratios used here: ING brand campaign lifestyle photography, 50mm f2, high-key natural morning sunlight through tall black-framed windows, Amsterdam canal-side workspace, pale wood and white-painted brick; real candid professionals, unposed; exactly one note of signature orange #FF6200 per frame; palette otherwise #0B0E12, #F4F2ED and desaturated warm neutrals; subject weighted right of frame, clear negative space in the left third; no text, no lettering, no logos, no readable screens. The black, white and orange grade is applied in CSS, not in the prompt, so every frame sits inside the palette rather than beside it.
